How to choose your tattoo placement and size

Tatties guide · Updated 2 November 2026

The design gets most of the attention, but where it goes and how big it is decide how it looks for the rest of your life. These are the things to weigh before you book, and how to check them before the needle does.

In short

Pick a spot you can live with in pain, visibility and how it ages, then size the design so its details still read in ten years. Look at it on your own body from every angle before you decide, and bring the placement and size to your artist: they will tell you what works for the design.

How much will it hurt?

Everyone feels it differently, and a long session hurts more than a short one in any spot. As a rule, places with more muscle or fat and fewer nerve endings are easier; thin skin over bone and very sensitive areas are harder.

  • Usually easier: outer upper arm, outer forearm, calf, outer thigh, shoulder and the fleshy part of the upper back.
  • In between: chest, inner forearm, upper back near the shoulder blades, front of the thigh.
  • Usually harder: ribs, sternum, spine, hips, knees and the back of the knee, elbows, armpit, inner upper arm, neck, hands, fingers, feet and ankles.

A first tattoo in an easier spot is a good way to learn how you handle it.

How it moves with your body

A tattoo is not a sticker on a flat surface. It sits on a body that curves, moves and changes.

  • Curves: on an arm or a leg a design wraps around. A wide piece on the forearm is partly out of view from the front, and a straight line on paper can look bent on a round calf.
  • Muscles: designs that follow the shape of a muscle look natural when you move; designs that cut across it can look distorted when you flex.
  • Changes in your body: the stomach, hips, chest, upper arms and thighs stretch most with weight change, muscle growth or pregnancy. A tattoo there changes shape with them.

How it ages on each spot

  • Hands, fingers and feet fade and blur fastest: the skin renews quickly and rubs against everything. Many artists warn that these need touch-ups.
  • Sun fades ink. Forearms, hands, neck and lower legs get the most of it; sunscreen keeps them sharp.
  • Lines spread a little over the years on every spot. Fine lines that sit close together slowly merge, which is why size matters (see below).
  • Protected spots such as the upper arm, back and thigh usually keep their sharpness longest.

Visibility: work and daily life

How visible a tattoo is depends on your job, your country and what you wear, so think about the next ten years, not just now.

  • Almost always visible: hands, fingers, neck, face and behind the ear.
  • Visible in short sleeves or shorts: forearm, lower leg, ankle and wrist.
  • Easy to cover: upper arm, shoulder, back, chest, ribs, stomach and thigh.

If your work has a dress code, a spot that you can show or hide by choice gives you the most freedom.

Getting the size right

Size is where most regrets come from: too small is a much more common complaint than too big.

  • Detail needs room. Small lettering, faces and fine shading need enough size to stay readable as the lines spread. If the design has a lot of detail, let your artist go bigger or simplify it.
  • Fit the body part. A design that fills the space it sits in (the outer forearm, the calf, a shoulder blade) usually looks better than one that floats in the middle of a big area.
  • Leave room for later. If you might add more, a sleeve or a back piece one day, place this one so it can become part of it.
  • Measure in centimetres, not by eye. A design that looks big on a screen is often small on an arm.

Try it on your own body first

Printing the design and holding it against your arm in the mirror only gets you so far. In Tatties you build a 3D body with your height, weight and build, and put the design on it before you book:

  • Take a photo of the design or a drawing and cut it out with your finger.
  • Place it on the body as a wishlist piece. It wraps around arms and legs like a real tattoo.
  • Move, turn and resize it with two fingers. The size shows in centimetres.
  • Turn the body to see it from every side, next to the tattoos you already have.
  • Share a picture or a spinning video, or a card with the design, the placement and the size for your artist.

Your artist has the final say on what works for the design and your skin, but you arrive knowing what you want.
